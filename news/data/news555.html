EU leaders fail to agree on €45 billion loan for Ukraine amid growing divisions

2026, November 27

  • EU leaders fail to agree on €45 billion loan for Ukraine amid growing divisions

    2026, November 27

    European Union / Ukraine / United States / Russia

    European Union leaders ended a lengthy summit in Brussels on November 27 without reaching agreement on the bloc's proposed 2028–2034 budget or a €45 billion financial loan intended to support Ukraine in 2027. The negotiations lasted more than 12 hours and exposed significant disagreements between member states over how European funds should be distributed and how Ukraine's continuing military and financial needs should be financed. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz pushed for all 27 member states to support the proposed assistance, while Hungary, Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Bulgaria expressed reservations about using European funds for military support.

    European Council President António Costa confirmed that negotiations would resume at the next summit scheduled for December 17–18. The failure to reach an agreement represents a setback for Ukraine, which continues to depend heavily on international financial assistance to maintain government operations and defend itself against Russia. Meanwhile, US President Donald Trump said he had urged Russian President Vladimir Putin to withdraw military aggression across Europe and indicated that Russia could face serious consequences if diplomatic efforts failed. The developments underline the growing differences between Washington, European governments and Moscow over how the war should end and what security guarantees Ukraine should receive.
