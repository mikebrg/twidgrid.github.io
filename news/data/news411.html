European Central Bank Holds Interest Rates as Lagarde Announces Departure

2026, October 29

  • European Central Bank Holds Interest Rates as Lagarde Announces Departure

    2026, October 29

    Germany / France / European Union

    The European Central Bank kept its key interest rate at 2.5% on 29 October, pausing its recent tightening cycle as policymakers assessed inflationary pressures and economic conditions across the euro area. The decision came alongside confirmation from ECB President Christine Lagarde that she would leave her position in February, ahead of France's presidential election next spring. European markets remained relatively steady, while investors continued to assess the impact of energy prices and borrowing costs on economic growth.

    Lagarde's departure will create an important leadership transition at one of the world's most influential central banks. Financial markets are watching for indications of a possible interest-rate increase in December, as policymakers balance persistent inflation risks against the potential damage of tighter monetary conditions to economic activity. The selection of her successor will also attract political attention because the ECB plays a central role in maintaining price stability across the eurozone.
